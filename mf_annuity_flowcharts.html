MUTUAL FUNDS

📦 Open-End Fund vs Closed-End Fund vs UIT

The exam loves to test what makes a "mutual fund" different from its lookalikes.
FeatureOpen-End FundClosed-End FundUIT
Shares issued Continuously, no limit Fixed # at IPO only Fixed # at inception
Trades at NAV only (forward pricing) Market price — can trade at premium or discount to NAV Bid/ask near NAV set by sponsor
Where it trades Redeemed directly with the fund Exchange-listed, bought/sold like a stock Bought from sponsor / secondary market
Management Actively managed, ongoing Actively managed, ongoing Unmanaged — fixed portfolio
Redeemable? Yes — redeemable security, forward-priced within 7 days No — sell on exchange instead Yes, at approx. NAV, or holds to maturity/termination
Leverage / borrowing Very limited Can use leverage (preferred stock, debt) None
🧠 Exam trap: "Trades above or below NAV" = closed-end fund, never an open-end fund. Open-end funds ALWAYS transact exactly at NAV (plus/minus sales charge) — that's the giveaway phrase.

🎯 Mutual Fund Share Class — Which One Fits?

A / B / C shares: the exam tests which one suits a given investment amount + holding period.
Client wants to buy a mutual fund
↓
How long will they hold it, and how much are they investing?
↓
Large $ / long-term
Class A
Front-end load, reduced by breakpoints — best for large purchases held long-term
Small $ / long-term
Class B
No front load, back-end CDSC declining over time, converts to A shares later
Short holding period
Class C
No/low front load, level annual 12b-1 fee, small/no CDSC — costliest if held long-term
🧠 Memory hook: "A = Ahead of time (pay load upfront), B = Back-end (pay to leave early), C = Continuous (level fee forever)." B shares are rarely the exam's "best" answer once C exists — regulators view B shares skeptically for most investors.

🧮 NAV, Sales Charge & Breakpoints

The formulas the exam will make you compute.
ConceptFormula / Rule
NAV per share (Total Assets − Total Liabilities) ÷ Shares Outstanding
Public Offering Price (POP) NAV ÷ (1 − Sales Charge %) — this is what the buyer pays
Sales Charge % (POP − NAV) ÷ POP
Breakpoint Sales charge % drops as $ invested rises (volume discount) — only on Class A shares
Rights of Accumulation Prior appreciation + new purchases count toward the next breakpoint
Letter of Intent (LOI) Commit to a breakpoint $ level within 13 months — can backdate 90 days
🚫 Breakpoint sale (violation): selling just under a breakpoint to earn a higher commission — always a prohibited practice, tested constantly.

💰 Mutual Fund Taxation

What gets taxed, when, and how.

Taxed to shareholder each year

  • Dividends distributed (even if automatically reinvested)
  • Capital gains distributions (short- or long-term, passed through)
  • Realized gain when shares are sold/redeemed (capital gain/loss)

Exam traps

  • Reinvested dividends are STILL taxable in the year received — no tax deferral just because you didn't take cash
  • Buying a dividend — purchasing right before the ex-date creates an immediate taxable event with no economic benefit (a prohibited/poor practice to recommend)
  • Fund itself avoids double taxation only if it qualifies as a regulated investment company (RIC) under Subchapter M — must distribute ≥90% of income

⏱️ Accumulation Phase vs Annuitization (Payout) Phase

The two-phase lifecycle every annuity moves through.
Premiums paid in
Lump sum or periodic payments purchase "accumulation units"
Accumulation Phase
Money grows tax-deferred; value tracked in accumulation units (variable) or stated rate (fixed)
Annuitize (elect payout)
Accumulation units converted to a FIXED number of "annuity units" — irrevocable choice of payout option
Annuitization / Payout Phase
Regular payments begin; # of annuity units is fixed, but $ value of each unit still floats with performance (variable)
🧠 Memory hook: "Accumulation = # of units VARIES as you buy in. Annuitization = # of units is FIXED once you annuitize, but the $ value per unit can still move." That single distinction is tested constantly.

🧾 Annuity Taxation

LIFO withdrawals vs the exclusion ratio — know which applies when.
LUMP-SUM / PARTIAL WITHDRAWAL

Before annuitizing — LIFO taxation

  • Last-In-First-Out: withdrawals come out of GAINS first
  • Gains taxed as ordinary income (not capital gains)
  • Withdrawals before age 59½ also hit with a 10% IRS penalty on the taxable portion
  • Only after all gains withdrawn does the tax-free return of principal (cost basis) come out
VS
ANNUITIZED PAYMENTS

After annuitizing — Exclusion Ratio

  • Each payment is split: part return of principal (tax-free), part earnings (taxable)
  • Exclusion ratio = cost basis ÷ expected total payout — sets the tax-free % of every check
  • Ratio applies consistently to each payment for the payout period
  • Once basis is fully recovered, 100% of further payments become taxable
🧠 Exam trap: non-qualified annuities always use the owner's cost basis (after-tax dollars) for these calculations — unlike a qualified plan, contributions were not tax-deductible going in, so only the GROWTH is ever taxed.

🚦 Annuity Sales — Suitability & Prohibited Practices

Annuities (esp. variable) are among the most heavily suitability-scrutinized products on the exam.

✅ CAN DO

  • Recommend a variable annuity for tax-deferred growth + lifetime income guarantee, if suitable
  • Use a 1035 exchange to move funds between annuity/life contracts tax-free — if it genuinely benefits the client
  • Charge surrender fees per contract disclosure (typically declining over 7–10 yrs)
  • Sell riders (death benefit, income guarantee) for an added cost, if disclosed and suitable

🚫 CANNOT DO

  • Recommend a variable annuity inside an already tax-deferred account (e.g., IRA) — redundant tax deferral, a classic unsuitable-sale trap
  • Push a 1035 exchange mainly to reset the surrender period / generate new commission with no client benefit
  • Sell to a client without assessing age, liquidity needs, time horizon — especially senior/vulnerable investors (heightened suitability + often a required senior-suitability form)
  • Ignore high surrender charges vs. client's near-term liquidity needs
  • Misrepresent a variable annuity's guarantees as risk-free — sub-account performance is NOT guaranteed
🧠 Exam trap: the #1 tested unsuitable annuity sale is "variable annuity recommended inside an IRA for the tax-deferral benefit" — the IRA is already tax-deferred, so that reasoning is a red flag every time it appears in a question stem.
