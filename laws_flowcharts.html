🧾 Who Registers Where

The 5 roles the exam loves to mix up.
RoleWhat they doRegisters with
Investment Adviser (IA) Firm/person giving investment advice for compensation SEC or State (by AUM — see flow above)
Investment Adviser Rep (IAR) Individual who works for an IA and gives the advice Always State
Broker-Dealer (BD) Firm that buys/sells securities for clients or its own account SEC + State (BDs register both, plus FINRA membership)
Agent (Registered Rep) Individual who works for a BD, executes trades State (per state they do business in)
Issuer Company that issues the securities being sold Not a BD or agent — the security itself may need registration, not the issuer as a person
🧠 Memory hook: "IA firm follows the money (AUM), IAR/Agent always follow the state." If you see "individual" in the question stem, the answer is almost always state.

⚖️ Fiduciary Duty (IA) vs Suitability (BD)

The single most-tested contrast in the Laws & Ethics section.
HIGHER STANDARD

Fiduciary Duty — owed by IAs / IARs

  • Must act in client's best interest, always
  • Duty of loyalty — client interests first, disclose/avoid conflicts
  • Duty of care — reasonable care + competence
  • Must disclose all material conflicts (comp, affiliations)
  • Ongoing duty — ​ ​ ​ applies for life of the advisory relationship
VS
LOWER STANDARD

Suitability — owed by BDs / Agents

  • Recommendation must be suitable for the client — not necessarily "best"
  • Based on client's investment profile (age, objectives, risk tolerance, time horizon)
  • No ongoing monitoring duty required after the trade
  • Conflicts don't have to be as fully disclosed as under fiduciary duty
  • Transaction-based — ​ ​ ​ applies at point of each recommendation
🧠 Exam trap: if the question asks what standard applies to an IA → fiduciary. If it's a BD/agent recommending a trade → suitability. "Best interest" language = fiduciary/IA. "Suitable" language = BD/agent.

🚦 What an Adviser/Agent CAN and CANNOT Do

Prohibited practices vs. permitted practices, side by side.

✅ CAN DO

  • Disclose a conflict of interest and proceed with client's informed consent
  • Charge performance fees — only to qualified clients (≥$1.1M AUM w/ adviser or ≥$2.2M net worth)
  • Use soft dollars (pay more in commissions for research) if it benefits clients and is disclosed
  • Trade on a discretionary account without asking every time — if written authorization is on file
  • Advise against a client's bad decision — client still has final say (it's their money)
  • Refer clients via a solicitor — if solicitor discloses the referral fee + delivers the IA's brochure
  • Seek best execution — doesn't have to be the cheapest broker, just the best overall outcome

🚫 CANNOT DO

  • Guarantee returns or guarantee against loss — always prohibited, no exceptions
  • Trade without authorization in a non-discretionary account
  • Churn — excessive trading just to generate commissions
  • Front-run — trade for own account ahead of a big client order
  • Insider trade — trade on material, non-public information
  • Sell away — sell securities not approved by the agent's own firm
  • Commingle — mix client funds/securities with firm or personal funds
  • Charge performance fees to retail clients (non-qualified)
  • Assign the advisory contract to another adviser without client consent
🧠 Exam trap: disclosure fixes MOST conflicts — but it never makes an illegal act (insider trading, guaranteeing returns) legal. If the question stem includes "guarantee," the answer is prohibited, full stop.

Remedy
Client recovers purchase price + interest, minus any income received

IA/BD generates client records, contracts, communications
3 years easily accessible
IA records must be readily accessible for the first 3 years
5 years total (IA) / 3 years (BD)
Total retention: IA = 5 years, BD = 3 years
🧠 Memory hook: "2 knows, 3 grows" — 2 years after you KNOW, 3 years after it GREW (happened) — whichever is sooner caps the client's right to sue. Recordkeeping: IA "5-3" (5 total, 3 easily accessible), BD flat 3.
