🧭 Which Bond Strategy Fits the Client's Goal?

Walk top to bottom — match the client's rate view + cash-flow need to a strategy.
Client holds a fixed-income portfolio
↓
What's the primary goal?
↓
Regular reinvestment + liquidity
Laddering
Stagger maturities evenly → principal returns periodically to reinvest at current rates
Flexibility, both short + long ends
Barbell
Hold only short-term + long-term (no middle) → liquidity from short end, yield from long end
Maximize yield at one known date
Bullet
Concentrate maturities around a single target date → funds a specific future liability
Guarantee a target value regardless of rates
Immunization
Match portfolio duration to the liability's time horizon → price risk and reinvestment risk offset
🧠 The exam's favorite tell: "expects rates to rise/fall and wants principal returned periodically to reinvest" → almost always laddering. "Matches a single future date/liability" → bullet or immunization depending on whether cash-flow timing matters (bullet) or only the end value matters (immunization).

📈 How Each Strategy Behaves by Rate Scenario

This is exactly the kind of "which strategy wins if rates do X" question the exam asks.

📉 Rates Expected to FALL

Best fit: Bullet or long Barbell leg

Lock in today's higher rates for longer — you don't want bonds maturing soon and forcing reinvestment at the new, lower rates. Laddering still reinvests some proceeds at the worse rate each rung.

📈 Rates Expected to RISE

Best fit: Laddering

You want principal returned periodically so it can be reinvested at the new, higher rates — this is the exact scenario in the sample question above. Long bullets/barbells lock you out of the upside.

❓ Rate Direction UNCERTAIN

Best fit: Laddering or Barbell

Both diversify across the curve so no single rate move dominates the outcome. Ladders smooth reinvestment risk continuously; barbells hedge by owning both extremes.

🎯 Must Fund a Known Future Liability

Best fit: Immunization (or Bullet)

When the goal is a guaranteed dollar amount at a specific date regardless of what rates do in between, duration-matching (immunization) is the textbook answer; a bullet works when cash-flow timing (not just ending value) matters.

⚠️ Common Exam Traps

✅ Correctly Match These Clues

  • "Concerned about price loss on long bonds + rates rising modestly + wants liquidity/reinvestment" → Laddering
  • "Wants some liquidity AND higher long-term yield, comfortable with a gap in the middle" → Barbell
  • "Needs $X at a specific date for a specific purpose" → Bullet
  • "Must guarantee a target portfolio value against a future liability no matter what rates do" → Immunization (duration matching)

⚠️ Don't Confuse These

  • Immunization is NOT the same as a bullet — immunization matches duration to a horizon and can hold a mix of maturities; a bullet clusters actual maturities at one date
  • A ladder does NOT maximize yield at any one point — it trades peak yield for steady, predictable reinvestment
  • A barbell is NOT the same as a ladder — barbell = concentrated at 2 ends; ladder = spread evenly across ALL points
  • Rising rates do NOT favor a bullet/barbell's long end — long-duration bonds lose the most value when rates rise
🧠 Retention hook: "LADDER = liquidity via periodic reinvestment. BARBELL = both ends, gap in the middle. BULLET = one date, one purpose. IMMUNIZED = duration-matched, rate-move-proof for a target value."
